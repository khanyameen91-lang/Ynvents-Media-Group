The Market

Where Denver events actually happen.

Denver's meeting business is unusually spread out — three distinct clusters, each with a different kind of event and a different load-in reality.

Downtown & the Convention Center

The Colorado Convention Center anchors the market's national association meetings and trade shows, with a dense ring of downtown hotels absorbing the overflow programming and evening events.

LoDo & Union Station

Lower Downtown carries the market's character venues — historic buildings, restored warehouses, and hotel spaces with real architectural personality and correspondingly awkward rigging.

The Tech Center & South

The Denver Tech Center corridor runs corporate meetings, sales kickoffs, and training programs out of business hotels built for exactly that — practical rooms, predictable builds, repeat calendars.

Representative Build

What a typical multi-day conference looks like.

Scoping is specific to your event, and yours will differ. But Denver draws a lot of association and corporate conferences with a main room plus concurrent breakouts, so here's how that shape gets built and staffed.

Scope Your Event
  • PlanningRoom-by-room package built off the agenda, not a flat per-day rate. A breakout with a panel needs different gear than one with a single presenter.
  • Load-inGeneral session built first and largest, breakouts staged behind it, all inside the venue's dock and freight-elevator window.
  • Main roomAudio tuned to the ballroom, stage wash and brand lighting set, screens or LED built and mapped to the presentation deck.
  • BreakoutsEach concurrent room gets its own package and its own technician — not one person covering three sessions and arriving late to all of them.
  • RehearsalPresenters run decks on the real switcher. Remote panelists, if any, are dialed in and leveled before doors.
  • Show daysOperators in every active room, a producer holding the master timeline, and comms open to your team and the venue throughout.
  • TurnaroundsRooms reset between sessions on the agenda's clock — the part of a multi-day build that quietly decides whether the event runs on time.

Denver FAQ

Questions we get from Denver planners.

Do you have a team and equipment based in Denver?

Yes to both. We have a full-time crew living and working in Denver and house inventory stocked in the market — not a team and a truck dispatched from Florida. We serve events across the Denver metro from that base, and both the crew and the inventory grow as we add properties.

What size events do you handle in Denver?

From a 12-person boardroom briefing to a 3,000-seat general session. Multi-day conferences with concurrent breakout rooms are a common shape for this market and are well within scope.

Can you support a conference with breakout rooms running at the same time?

Yes. Concurrent rooms are a staffing and scheduling problem before they're an equipment problem, and they're scoped that way — each room gets its own package and a technician who isn't being shared across three sessions.

Can you stream a Denver event to remote attendees?

Yes. Virtual and hybrid production is a core service line, with the room mix and the stream designed as one system. More on virtual & hybrid.

We manage a Denver hotel. Do you take on in-house AV partnerships?

Yes — this is exactly how we grow a market. The model is white-label and revenue-share: our technicians operate under your brand, with no equipment investment on your side. See how partnerships work.
